गुरुर्ब्रह्मा गुरुर्विष्णुः
गुरुर्देवो महेश्वरः ।
गुरुः साक्षात्परब्रह्म
तस्मै श्रीगुरवे नमः ॥
गुरवे सर्वलोकानां भिषजे भवरोगिणाम् ।
निधये सर्वविद्यानां दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः ॥
हरिः ॐ । ॐ श्री गुरुभ्यो नमः। हरिः ॐ ।

Let us start, let us first chant the śānti mantra and then the first pañcati of Aruṇam.
भ॒द्र-ङ्कर्णे॑भि-श्शृणु॒याम॑ देवाः । 
भ॒द्र-म्प॑श्येमा॒क्षभि॒-र्यज॑त्राः । 
स्थि॒रैरङ्गै᳚स्तुष्टु॒वाग्ं स॑स्त॒नूभिः॑ । 
व्यशे॑म दे॒वहि॑तं॒-यँदायुः॑ । 
स्व॒स्ति न॒ इन्द्रो॑ वृ॒द्धश्र॑वाः । 
स्व॒स्ति नः॑ पू॒षा वि॒श्ववे॑दाः । 
स्व॒स्ति न॒स्तार्क्ष्यो॒ अरि॑ष्टनेमिः । 
स्व॒स्ति नो॒ बृह॒स्पति॑-र्दधातु । 
ॐ शान्ति॒-श्शान्ति॒-श्शान्तिः॑ ॥

[Then repeated as part of the first pañcati:]
भ॒द्र-ङ्कर्णे॑भि-श्शृणु॒याम॑ देवाः । 
भ॒द्र-म्प॑श्येमा॒क्षभि॒-र्यज॑त्राः । 
स्थि॒रैरङ्गै᳚स्तुष्टु॒वाग्ं स॑स्त॒नूभिः॑ । 
व्यशे॑म दे॒वहि॑तं॒-यँदायुः॑ । 
स्व॒स्ति न॒ इन्द्रो॑ वृ॒द्धश्र॑वाः । 
स्व॒स्ति नः॑ पू॒षा वि॒श्ववे॑दाः । 
स्व॒स्ति न॒स्तार्क्ष्यो॒ अरि॑ष्टनेमिः । 
स्व॒स्ति नो॒ बृह॒स्पति॑-र्दधातु । 
आप॑मापाम॒प-स्सर्वाः᳚ । 
अ॒स्मा-द॒स्मा-दि॒तो-ऽमुतः॑ ॥ 1 ॥

So we have seen the meaning of the śānti mantra, why we say three times “śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ” – it is for the tāpatraya nivāraṇārtham, freedom from obstacles of ādhyātmika, ādhibhautika, ādhidaivika. Now the second time, this mantra is repeated as part of the first pañcati, because specifically we are asking nivāraṇam, removing of obstacles, in doing this adhyayanam or pārāyaṇam of Aruṇam.
Now let us see the meaning of every word.
भद्रं कर्णेभिः शृणुयाम देवाः
Bhadrám means auspicious, good, noble. Karṇebhiḥ – with the ears. Śṛṇuyāma – “may we hear”. Devāḥ – O Devas. Here “Devāḥ” is generally addressed, but we can also interpret it as those particular devatās mentioned below in the mantra, like Indra, Pūṣan, Tārkṣya and Bṛhaspati. So: “O Devas, may we always hear auspicious things through our ears; please bless us so that we can always hear auspicious things.”
भद्रं पश्येमाक्षभिर्यजत्राः
Bhadrám – auspicious. Paśyema – let us see. Akṣabhiḥ – through our eyes. Yajatrāḥ – those who are worthy of worship, addressing the Devas again as those worthy of worship; it can also mean “those who are doing the worship”, but in this context the first meaning fits better. So: “O worship worthy Devas, please bless us to always see auspicious things.”
स्थिरैरङ्गैस्तुष्टुवाँसस्तनूभिः
Sthiraiḥ means very firm, very strong. Aṅgaiḥ – limbs, parts of the body. Tuṣṭuvāṃsaḥ – worshipping, praising. Tanūbhiḥ – bodies. All these are in plural, because the prayer is not just for oneself individually, but collectively for everyone who is chanting or doing the adhyayanam of Aruṇam. So: “O Devas, let us worship you with strong, firm limbs, that is, with a healthy body.”
व्यशेम देवहितं यदायुः
Vyaśema – let us obtain, may we live out, may we attain. Devahitam – as ordained by the gods, what is fixed by the Devas as our lifespan. Yad āyuḥ – that length of life, that much life span. “How much life we could live is already ordained, already decided; may we live that fully and healthily, without issues.”
Here the speaker notes śāstra statements about lifespan: some places speak of 100 years (as in the blessing “śatamānaṃ bhavati śatāyuḥ”), elsewhere 116 years as pūrṇa āyuṣ, and in some places 120 years, which matches well with some calendrical considerations. He links this to the 60 year cycle based on Bṛhaspati’s motion, saying Bṛhaspati takes about 12 years to go once around the zodiac, and five such cycles make 60 years; the traditional year names (from Prabhava onwards) follow this Bṛhaspati mānaṃ. When we celebrate ṣaṣṭyabda pūrti at 60, the birth year as per Bṛhaspati mānaṃ comes again, which is why the rite is done then. All this is used to illustrate “devahitaṃ yad āyuḥ” – “may we live that full divinely ordained span of life in good health.”
स्वस्ति न इन्द्रो वृद्धश्रवाः
Svasti means auspiciousness, maṅgalam, kalyāṇam. Naḥ – to us. Indraḥ – Lord Indra, Deva Indra. Vṛddha śravaḥ – one of great fame; śravaḥ = fame, vṛddha = great. “May Indra of great glory bless us with well being.”
स्वस्ति नः पूषा विश्ववेदाः
Again svasti – auspiciousness; naḥ – to us. Pūṣā – one form of Sūrya, among the twelve Ādityas, the nourisher (poṣayati – one who nourishes). Viśva vedāḥ – he who knows everything in this universe. “May Pūṣan, knower of all, also bless us with well being.”
स्वस्तिनस्तार्क्ष्योऽरिष्टनेमिः
Again svasti – auspiciousness; naḥ – to us. Tārkṣyaḥ – Garuḍa Bhagavān. Ariṣṭa nemiḥ – ariṣṭa means “without injury, without harm” (ahiṃsā), nemi is the metal rim or band at the circumference of a chariot wheel that keeps it intact so it runs smoothly; thus ariṣṭa nemiḥ is “he who removes obstacles on the path, allowing a smooth journey.” Garuḍa, who travels swiftly without obstacles and serves as the vehicle of Śrī Mahā Viṣṇu, is thus prayed to: “May that obstacle removing Garuḍa bless us so that there are no obstacles on our path of adhyayanam.”
स्वस्तिनो बृहस्पतिर्दधातु
Svasti – auspiciousness; no – to us. Bṛhaspatiḥ – the Deva guru, lord of wisdom. Dadhātu – may he give, may he bestow. “Let Bṛhaspati, the preceptor of the gods, grant us blessings, especially medhā-śakti, so that we remember the mantras and do not forget them in our adhyayanam.”
So up to here, it is the same śānti mantra, but in the standard recitation it ends with “ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः”. In the pañcati, however, it continues into the next part addressing the waters.
आपो मा अपाम् अपः सर्वाः
Āpaḥ – waters. Mā – unto me (or us). Apām apaḥ – explained from tradition as “āpām apām plus apaḥ”: “again and again waters.” Sarvāḥ – all, from everywhere. So: “May all waters, from everywhere, come to me / to us; may there be no scarcity of water.” Swami Omkārānanda is quoted here as glossing it as “āpām apām plus apaḥ – again and again, may we obtain water.”
Water is highlighted because, among the pañca bhūtas, water is especially essential for all rituals: every japa and karma begins with water, doing ācamana, invoking the seven sacred rivers into the water, doing sprinkling (prokṣaṇa) and śuddhi with water, etc.
अस्मा अदस्मादितोऽमुतः
Asmāt asmāt – from here and from here again; ihaḥ – from this world (bhūmi); amutah – from the other world, such as svarga loka. In the explanation: “asmāt, asmāt iha amutah – from here and there, from this world and the other world.” So the sense is: “May we obtain waters from here and from there, from earth and from higher worlds, from all sides, so there is no scarcity of water.”
The speaker concludes: this is the meaning of the first pañcati; in short we are praying—
to always hear good things,
to always see good things,
to worship with a healthy body,
to live our full destined lifespan healthily so we can worship,
and to be blessed by Indra, Pūṣan, Tārkṣya (Garuḍa) and Bṛhaspati,
then the mantra shifts to worship of waters, asking that water be abundantly available for all our rituals and life.
He notes that more on “why waters” and further details will be taken up from the next pañcati onward.
Closing
ॐ पूर्णमदः पूर्णमिदं पूर्णात्पूर्णमुदच्यते ।
पूर्णस्य पूर्णमादाय पूर्णमेवावशिष्यते ॥
ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः ॥
हरिः ॐ । ॐ श्री गुरुभ्यो नमः। हरिः ॐ ।
