The second pañcati of a Vedic text is focusing on the ritual significance and philosophical nature of water. It serves as a continuation of a previous session where waters from all sources were symbolically "collected" for the performance of the Aruṇaketuka yajña.
The Role of Elements and Divine Support
The transcription emphasizes that water is a critical prerequisite for any anuṣṭhānam (ritual), including pūjā, yajña, and purification rites like ācamana. In this second pañcati, the practitioner invokes major deities to energize these waters:
Agni, Vāyu, and Sūrya: These deities are requested to bless the ritual and provide the intent (dhiyā) needed to infuse the waters with mantra for growth and prosperity.
Vāyu as the Carrier: Vāyu (the wind) is likened to horses carrying water in the form of clouds, while Sūrya (the sun) acts as the "lord of the rays" protecting them.
The Inner Principle: Agni is described as the ātman (soul) of water. This is supported by the cosmological sequence where water is born from fire (tejas), and the principle of Pañcīkaraṇa, which states that every element contains a portion of the others.
Divine Blessings and Progeny
The waters are addressed as Jalābhimāni devatās (divine goddesses) who are both harmless and beneficial (adrugāḥ). They are recognized as the "creators of the world" (bhuvana-suvarīḥ) because, according to traditional texts like the Tattvabodha, the earth arises from water. The pañcati concludes with a specific prayer for progeny (putravat), requesting that the waters grant descendants so that dharmic practices may continue through future generations.
