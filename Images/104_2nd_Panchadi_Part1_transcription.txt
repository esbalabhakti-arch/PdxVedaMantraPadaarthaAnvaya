गुरुर्ब्रह्मा गुरुर्विष्णुः
गुरुर्देवो महेश्वरः ।
गुरुः साक्षात्परब्रह्म
तस्मै श्रीगुरवे नमः ॥
गुरवे सर्वलोकानां भिषजे भवरोगिणाम् ।
निधये सर्वविद्यानां दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः ॥
हरिः ॐ । ॐ श्री गुरुभ्यो नमः। हरिः ॐ ।

We have seen the first pañcati in the last recording. Now let us chant the second pañcati.
अ॒ग्निर्वा॒युश्च॒ सूर्य॑श्च । 
स॒ह स॑ञ्च-स्क॒रर्धि॑या । 
वा॒य्वश्वा॑ रश्मि॒पत॑यः । 
मरी᳚च्यात्मानो॒ अद्रु॑हः । 
दे॒वी-र्भु॑वन॒ सूव॑रीः । 
पु॒त्र॒व॒त्वाय॑ मे सुत ।
(The speaker chants only up to “पु॒त्र॒व॒त्वाय॑ मे सुत ।” here; “महानाम्नी-र्म॑हामा॒नाः । म॒ह॒सो म॑हस॒-स्स्वः॑ । दे॒वीः प॑र्जन्य॒ सूव॑रीः । पु॒त्र॒व॒त्वाय॑ मे सुत ॥” are previewed and then explained later as part of the same pañcati, but the detailed explanation of that half is said to continue in the next recording.)

In the previous pañcati, all waters were “collected”: “आप॑मापाम॒प-स्सर्वाः᳚ । अ॒स्मा-द॒स्मा-दि॒तो-ऽमुतः॑ ” – gathering waters from everywhere.
Waters are described as critical for any anuṣṭhānam and also for health: without water no pūjā or yajña is possible; the sequence begins with ācamana, then sthala-śuddhi, kalaśa-sthāpanam, invoking devatās in the kalaśa, and pūjā, āhvāna of Rudra or other devatās, puṇyāhavācana, śuddhi – all using water.
Hence first the waters are praised and collected; in the second pañcati, devas like Agni, Vāyu and Sūrya are worshipped and requested to help in gathering and energizing these waters.
“अ॒ग्निर्वा॒युश्च॒ सूर्य॑श्च । स॒ह स॑ञ्च-स्क॒रर्धि॑या । ”
Agnir vāyuś ca sūryaś ca – Agni (fire god), Vāyu (wind god) and Sūrya (sun god).
“Let Agni, Vāyu and Sūrya bless us here.”
Saha sañcaskāra-dhiyā – saha “together with them”, sañcaskāre “in this ritual action / consecratory act”, dhiyā “with this intent or resolve”.
The sense given: “With these waters that we collected, together with Agni, Vāyu, Sūrya, I am going to perform this Aruṇa ketuka yajña; I am going to infuse mantra into these waters, to make them more energetic and perform this energetic yajña, so please bless me.”
R̥ddhyai (implied in the explanation as “for r̥ddhi”) – for abundance, growth, prosperity.
So the opening line means:
“With the help and blessings of Agni, Vāyu and Sūrya, using the collected waters, may this Aruṇa ketuka yajña be performed for prosperity and growth.”
“वा॒य्वश्वा॑ रश्मि॒पत॑यः…”
Vāyus is the wind; aśvāḥ (here in the compound vaivasvā) are horses.
Vāyvasvā rasmipatayāḥ is explained as: Vāyu is like the horses carrying the cloud forms of water, and the rāśmī-pati (“lord of the rays”) is Sūrya, whose rays protect and guide those waters.
In imagery: when waters become clouds and move everywhere, the wind moves them, as if the water deities ride on horses that are Vāyu himself, and sun rays guard those clouds.
Thus this line praises the Jalābhimāni devatās as being carried by Vāyu and protected by the rays of Sūrya.
“मारीच्यात्मानोऽद्रुगाः…”
Adrugāḥ – harmless, beneficial, not causing injury.
So the waters are described as non injurious, benevolent, only blessing us.
Mārīcy-ātmanaḥ – “having Marīci as their ātmā”. Marīci is both a name associated with Agni and also one of the Dvādaśa Ādityas (a name of Sūrya).
Agni is explained as the ātman in the waters: the waters have Agni as their inner principle. This is unpacked using the cosmological sequence from Tattva bodha and Taittirīya Upaniṣad:
“ततः आकाशः सम्भूतः, आकाशात् वायुः, वायोस्तेजः, तेजसः आपः, अद्भ्यः पृथ्वी” – from ākāśa comes Vāyu, from Vāyu Agni (tejas), from Agni waters, from waters earth.
Thus waters are born from Agni, so Agni can be called their inner principle.
Panchīkaraṇa is also invoked: the tāmasic portion of each gross element is mixed so that each element contains portions of the other four; for instance, water is half “water” plus one eighth each of space, air, fire and earth. In that sense too, Agni is present in water.
Hence: “Mārīci is the ātman of the waters; they are harmless, beneficent waters whose soul is fire / Sūrya.”
“दे॒वी-र्भु॑वन॒ सूव॑रीः । पु॒त्र॒व॒त्वाय॑ मे सुत ”
Devyaḥ – addressed here to the Jalābhimāni devatās, the divine waters, as goddesses.
This is compared with the prokṣaṇa mantra in Sandhyāvandanam: “आपो हि ष्ठा मयोभुवस्ता न ऊर्जे दधातन …” where waters are similarly invoked as divine and life giving.
Bhuvana-suvarīḥ – creators or sustainers of this bhuvanam (world, earth). Recalling “adbhyah pṛthvī” from Tattva bodha, the world arises from waters; thus waters are the source of the earth we live on.
So: “O divine waters, you are the cause/creators of this world we inhabit.”
Putravat tvaya me sutāḥ (glossed as putravatvayā me sutaḥ): putravatvayā means “for having progeny”, being blessed with children; me – for me; sutāḥ – “bless (us)”. The speaker clarifies that sutāḥ here does not mean “son” but “bless, grant”.
So the request is: “O waters, please bless me / us with progeny, so that dharmic practices (dharmānushtāna) continue across generations, carried onward by children and their descendants.”
The remaining half of the pañcati (“महानाम्नी-र्म॑हामा॒नाः । म॒ह॒सो म॑हस॒-स्स्वः॑ । दे॒वीः प॑र्जन्य॒ सूव॑रीः । पु॒त्र॒व॒त्वाय॑ मे सुत ॥”) is mentioned but its detailed explanation is deferred by the speaker to the next recording; he states he will continue and then summarize the entire pañcati.
