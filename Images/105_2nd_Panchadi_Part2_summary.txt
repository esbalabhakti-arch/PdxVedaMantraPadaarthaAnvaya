This summary explores the exegetical commentary on the second Panchadi of the Aruna Ketuka Chayanam (Aruna Parayanam). The discourse focuses on the metaphysical and ritualistic significance of water (Apaha) and its essential role in Vedic liturgy.

Ritual Framework and Elemental Integration
The commentary begins by situating the practitioner within a triad of solar and elemental forces: Agni (fire), Vayu (wind), and Surya (sun). These deities facilitate the Sanchaskara, the ritual preparation intended to bring Ruddhi (prosperity). The waters are personified as "harmless" (Adruga) yet dynamic, characterized by the speed of horses and vitalized by the solar rays and the essence of Agni.

The Primacy of Water in Divinity
A central theme is the designation of water as Mahanamni (greatly named) and Mahamana (highly revered). The text posits that water is the fundamental medium for all Vedic worship; regardless of the specific deity being invoked—be it Vishnu, Varuna, or Shiva—the divine essence is first channeled through the Kalasam (ritual water vessel).

Cosmological and Regenerative Functions
The commentary distinguishes between two aspects of the waters:

Bhuvana-suvari: Water as the primordial cause of the universe.

Parjanya-suvari: Water as the source of rain and atmospheric fertility.

The discourse concludes with a petition for Putravatvaya (progeny), framing the desire for descendants not merely as personal gain, but as a necessity for the perpetual preservation of Dharma.
