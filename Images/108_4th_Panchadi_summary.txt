The discourse begins by identifying four Pramanams (means of valid knowledge) or Sadhanams (tools) necessary to understand truths that are beyond direct sensory perception. These are collectively referred to as the Chatushtayam:

1)Smritihi: The Dharma Shastras or codes of conduct codified by Rishis, such as Manusmriti.
2)Pratyaksham (Shrutihi): While literally meaning direct perception via the senses, in this context, it refers to the Vedas (Shruti) as the highest form of direct auditory revelation.
3)Aidihyam: The historical and mythological traditions found in the Itihasas and Puranas.
4)Anumanah: Logical inference derived from other established sources of knowledge.

The text asserts that the Aditya Mandalam (the solar orb) cannot be fully grasped through human Indriyas (senses) alone. Instead, it must be understood through these four systematic tools of inquiry.

Surya as the Architect of Time:
The central theological argument posits that Surya shines his rays (Marichi) from above all worlds. The interaction of these rays with the physical world is described through the concept of Paka Visheshana. While Paka literally means "cooking," in this philosophical context, it signifies the transformative energy of solar heat.

This transformation is the catalyst for Kala Visheshanam—the differentiation of time. The text explains that the physical changes observed in nature—such as the growth of a seed into a tree or the transition of seasons (summer and winter)—are the "cooking" effects of solar energy. Thus, Surya is the cause of the Upadhis (limiting adjuncts or conditions) that allow humans to measure and experience time. The summary distinguishes this from absolute time, which is without attributes and beyond human experience.

The Eternal Flow of Time:
Finally, the text employs a river metaphor to describe the nature of Vyavaharika Kalam. Just as a river (Nadi) flows continuously and inexhaustibly (Akshaya) from its source (Prabhavat), time streams forth from Surya in an unending cycle of day and night. This establishes the sun as an inexhaustible fountainhead of the temporal order.
