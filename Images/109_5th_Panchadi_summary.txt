This text provides an exegetical analysis of the fifth Panchadi from the second Anuvakam of the Taittiriya Aranyaka. The summary focuses on the metaphorical and metaphysical relationship between fragmented time and the totality of the solar year.

The Macro-Micro River Analogy
The primary pedagogical device used in this verse is the comparison of a great river to the Samvatsara (the year). Just as small, tributary rivers (Nadyah) originate from manifold sources and merge into a singular, vast river, the various divisions of time arise and converge into the year. This influx of smaller units makes the river of time Urusati (vast/wide), ensuring it flows forward continuously without retreating (Na Nivartate).

Classification of Temporal Units
The text categorizes time through the lens of Anusascha and Mahasascha:

Anusahah: The microscopic or "atomic" units of time, such as Kshanam (moment) and Nimisham (blink of an eye).

Mahasahah: The macroscopic divisions, including Paksham (fortnight), Masam (month), and Ritus (seasons).

The Samvatsara is described as Samavishtahah, meaning it encompasses and is filled with all these various divisions. They do not merely exist independently but "rest upon" or "depend upon" (Shrutahah) the structural framework of the year.

The Adhidevata: Sun as the Presiding Deity
The philosophical conclusion of the verse centers on the recognition of the Adhisamvatsaram—the supervising or presiding deity of time. The text posits that through the observation of these characteristics (Lakshane)—the convergence of small units into a vast, irreversible flow—one should understand the Sun as the primary intelligence and governing force behind the Samvatsara.

In this framework, the Sun serves as the ultimate source and container for the diverse fragments of temporal experience, unifying them into a singular, vast stream of cosmic order.
