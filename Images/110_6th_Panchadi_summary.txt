The Sixth Panchadi and the Metaphysics of Time
The sixth Panchadi of the second Anuvakam provides a profound ontological exploration of time, transitioning from the observable units of the calendar to the underlying metaphysical reality of the Kala Purusha (the Personification of Time) or Paramatma. The text contrasts direct sensory perception (Pratyaksha) with the hidden causal truths that govern the cosmic cycle.

The Perception of Samvatsara
The passage begins by reaffirming the structure of time through its constituent parts, which are categorized into two primary scales:
Anubihi: Micro-units of time, such as Kshanam (moment) and Nimisham (blink of an eye).
Magadbhihi: Macro-units of time, including Paksham (fortnight) and Masam (month).
While the Samvatsara (the year) is directly perceived through these units, the text notes a significant epistemological limitation: humanity experiences the "year" but fails to see the Adisatvaha, the underlying truth or causal substance behind it.

The Barrier of Sensory Perception
A unique metaphorical framework is employed to explain why the ultimate cause of time remains hidden. The text introduces the terms Patarah (clothing/eyelids), Viklidah (moist), and Pingah (reddish-brown) to describe the physical eyes.

The Protective Veil: Just as clothing protects the body, the eyelids protect the eyes; however, in this context, the physical apparatus of sight acts as a veil.

The Solar Illusion: While the eyes perceive the physical sun—characterized by its golden-brown hue—as the source of the year, they are incapable of perceiving the Kala Purusha residing behind the visible orb.

Varuna Lakshanam: This limitation is described as the mark of Varuna, suggesting that our physical perception is "covered" or restricted to the material world.

Anatomy of the Kala Purusha
To bridge the gap between the visible and the invisible, the Arunam describes the structural "anatomy" of the Kala Purusha, symbolizing the totality of the temporal cycle:
Siraha (The Head): The Samvatsara itself constitutes the single head of the Time-Spirit.
Nana Mukhe (The Faces): Though the text mentions "many faces," it is traditionally interpreted as two: Uttarayanam and Dakshinayanam (the northern and southern solstitial movements).
Krishnam Ritu Lakshanam (The Characteristics): The six Ritus (seasons) are the defining marks or "limbs" that complete the whole, providing the diverse characteristics of the year.

Conclusion
Ultimately, the sixth Panchadi emphasizes that while the divisions of time are recorded and known through physical observation, such perception is inherently limited. True understanding requires moving beyond the "clothing" of the physical world to recognize the singular, complete entity of the Kala Purusha who encompasses all solstices, seasons, and moments.
