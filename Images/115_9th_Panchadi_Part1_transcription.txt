गुरुर्ब्रह्मा गुरुर्विष्णुः
गुरुर्देवो महेश्वरः ।
गुरुः साक्षात्परब्रह्म
तस्मै श्रीगुरवे नमः ॥
गुरवे सर्वलोकानां भिषजे भवरोगिणाम् ।
निधये सर्वविद्यानां दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः ॥
अस्मिन् गुरु चरणारविन्दाभ्याम् नमः।​

So we have seen so far eight Panchadis. Now let us look at the ninth Panchadi.
​
न हि प्र॒वेद॑ सुकृ॒तस्य॒ पन्था॒मिति॑ । ऋ॒तुर्-ऋ॑तुना नु॒द्यमा॑नः । 
विन॑नादा॒भिधा॑वः । षष्टिश्च त्रिग्ंश॑का व॒ल्गाः । 
शु॒क्लकृ॑ष्णौ च॒ षाष्टि॑कौ । सा॒रा॒ग॒व॒स्त्रै-र्ज॒रद॑क्षः । 
व॒स॒न्तो वसु॑भि-स्स॒ह । सं॒​वँ॒थ्स॒रस्य॑ सवि॒तुः । 
प्रै॒ष॒कृ-त्प्र॑थ॒म-स्स्मृ॑तः । अ॒मूना॒दय॑-तेत्य॒न्यान् ॥ 9 ॥

So let us look at line by line the meanings of the ninth Panchadi.
​
“न हि प्र॒वेद॑ सुकृ॒तस्य॒ पन्था॒मिति॑".
​
“Na” – not; “hi” – indeed.
​“Praveda” – “Praveda” means “knows fully”, he knows fully, clearly knows, Praveda.
“Sukrutasya” – Sukrutasya, that is something that is well done, very well done; Sukrutasya – very good action, punyam. In this case Sukrutasya is the punyam.
“Pantham” – the path.
“Iti” – in this manner, thus.
​
So literally it means – basically you have to connect this with the previous eighth Panchadi, the mantra that we saw: “यदीग्ं॑ शृ॒णोत्य॒लकग्ं॑ शृणोति…”, we saw, we saw that. So you have a friend who doubts about his true friend is not what, even if he learns the Vedas it’s in vain – we saw that in the last Panchadi, eighth Panchadi. So it’s a continuation of this one. It says here: the one who doubts, who has no faith in śāstras, will not know the path for the Puṇyaloka – that’s what it says here. The one who doubts, who does not have faith in the śāstras, will not know – “Nahi Praveda”, surely he will not know, “Nahi Praveda Sukrutasya Pantham”, the path to the Puṇyaloka. So he will not reach the Puṇyaloka, the one who doubts the śāstras – that’s what this really means.
​
So the next few mantras talks about the Ritus – how the Ritus are defined in our śāstra.
“ऋ॒तुर्-ऋ॑तुना नु॒द्यमा॑नः ” – Rutuḥ, Rutur, Rutumana, Rutuḥ, Rutumana, Rutuḥ – the seasons, the seasons; two months is one Ritu, we already saw that.​
“ऋ॒तुर्-ऋ॑तुना” – by the season; ऋ॒तुर्-ऋ॑तुना – the season by the season.
​“नु॒द्यमा॑नः” – it’s induced or proceeds. The season follows the previous season as if it is induced by it, or the season pulls the other season forward – that’s what this means: “ऋ॒तुर्-ऋ॑तुना नु॒द्यमा॑नः”.
​
“विन॑नादा॒भिधा॑वः” – Vinad, Vināda, Vināda Abhidavah. “Vināda Abhidavah” – Vināda means sounding, making sound, sounding. “Abhidavah” means running forward, rushing forward. So the season comes with nādam, with the sound, one after another. So every season is defined here as if to have its own unique sound – that’s what this mantra says.
​
“षष्टिश्च त्रिग्ंश॑का व॒ल्गाः” – Ṣaṣṭiḥ ca, Ṣaṣṭiḥ ca, Ṣaṣṭiś ca. “Ṣaṣṭiḥ” – 60, 60; “ca” – and. “Triṃśakāḥ” – Triṃśakāḥ means groups of 30, groups of 30. So the 30 here means the Muhurtas. One Muhurta is considered as two and a half nāḷigai – we will see those details later, how the time is measured in our śāstras; we will have a separate recording on that. But here just take it: so a day – or it says here group of 30s – there are 30 Muhurtas in a day, you know, as per our śāstra. So typically a day is called as a Triṃśakam – Triṃśakam means a day, and the day has 30 Muhurtas in it, that is Triṃśakāḥ. “Valgāḥ” – Valgāḥ means a samūham, a group, samūham grouped together, Valgāḥ. So “षष्टिश्च त्रिग्ंश॑का व॒ल्गाः” means the Ritus has a day of 30 Muhurtas in it, and there are 60 such days in a Ritu – that’s what this means.
​
We’ll see more in the next mantra what it says: “शु॒क्लकृ॑ष्णौ च॒ षाष्टि॑कौ”.
​“Śukla” – Śukla we know, it’s bright, white colour, bright. “Kṛṣṇa” is the Kṛṣṇa – dark or black. “Śukla Kṛṣṇau, शु॒क्लकृ॑ष्णौ च॒ षाष्टि॑कौ, शु॒क्लकृ॑ष्णौ च॒”. “Śukla Kṛṣṇau” means the pair of twos. Which pair? The bright and the dark, and the day and night – Śukla Kṛṣṇau. Here it means the Pakṣam – “Śukla Kṛṣṇau ca”, here it means the Pakṣam; the Pakṣam means 15 days, right? So we have Śukla Pakṣam, we have Kṛṣṇa Pakṣam. “षाष्टि॑कौ” – two sets of 60, two sets of 60.
​
So we already saw the Muhurtam – a day has 60 Muhurtams in it – and we have 60 such days in a Ritu. Now it just only talks about day there, not the nights. So here “षाष्टि॑कौ” means 60 days and 60 nights – so the two sets. So each Ritu thus have 60 days and 60 nights, षाष्टि॑कौ, consisting of two Śukla Pakṣas and two Kṛṣṇa Pakṣas, right? So in a month, if you look at it, we saw already two months makes one Ritu. So each month has one Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa and one Śukla Pakṣa; so that two months – so the two Kṛṣṇa Pakṣas in two months, and two Śukla Pakṣas in two months.
​
Also we should note here: a day as per śāstra is considered from Sūryodaya to Sūryāstamana – that is a day. And the night is from Sūryāstamana to the next Sūryodaya. So combining these two, day plus night is what we call as one English day – the 24 hours, right? For us it is: one full day as per śāstra is Sūryodaya to the next day Sūryodaya – that’s called as full one day. And within that we have 30 Muhurtas for the day and 30 Muhurtas for the night – okay? So 60 Muhurtas is one full day as per our śāstras.
​
So up to this mantra we saw the Ritus follow each other without any gap – that’s what it says: the Ritus pulls the other Ritu forward, so there is no gap in between them. Say, for example, like Vasant Ritu – after the Vasant Ritu, Grīṣma Ritu starts immediately, there’s no gap, right? So that’s what it says: Vasant Ritu pulls the Grīṣma Ritu – that’s what it means by the “ऋ॒तुर्-ऋ॑तुना नु॒द्यमा॑नः”, as if it pulls them.
​
And also each Ritu has their own svabhāvam, Rūpam – that we already saw in the eighth Panchadi; they all have their own Rūpam, their own svabhāvam – the nature is different between each Ritu. And this mantra adds one more to that: it says it also has a sound – “Vinanāda”, we saw, it also has a sound as well. For example, if you look at the Varṣā Ritu, it has more thunder, thunder storms, everything happens in the Varṣā Ritu more than any other Ritus.
