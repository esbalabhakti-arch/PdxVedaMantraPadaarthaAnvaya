The speaker continues explaining the ninth pañcati, focusing on how it characterizes Vasant ṛtu (spring) and then summarizing what the entire pañcati contributes to the description of seasons and time, still tied to the earlier theme of faith in śāstra.

He starts from the phrase rendered as “सा॒रा॒ग॒व॒स्त्रै-र्ज॒रद॑क्षः” Here, “rāga” is explained as colour or hue, and “vastraiḥ” as garments or clothes. “Saragavastraiḥ” is thus interpreted as: the season appears as if covered with coloured garments, specifically red. The season in question is clarified as Vasant ṛtu. The text is therefore poetically describing spring as if it arrives dressed in red cloth. This imagery is later connected to the transition from the preceding Śiśira ṛtu (late winter): after a cold, relatively colourless period, the onset of Vasant brings blossoms and vivid colours, making “red‑clothed” an apt metaphor.

The next word, “jaradakṣāḥ,” is taken to highlight the quality of water in Vasant. The speaker explains that waters in this season are very pure and clear, as though the season itself has purified them. So, taken together, the line conveys that Vasant ṛtu begins as if clothed in red garments and accompanied by clean, clear water sources.

The following phrase is “व॒स॒न्तो वसु॑भि-स्स॒ह” – “Vasant together with the Vasus.” Vasant ṛtu is said to come “with” the Vasus (Aṣṭa Vasus). The Vasus are introduced as a group of deities responsible for the sukha and general welfare of beings (prajās). The speaker lists them, with a brief gloss on what they represent:

Dhara – earth
Āpa – water
Anala – fire
Anila – wind
Pratyūṣa – dawn
Prabhāsa – light

He notes he is temporarily forgetting two names, but emphasizes that collectively these Aṣṭa Vasus embody fundamental natural elements and conditions. The point is that after Śiśira, in Vasant, these elemental forces are more balanced and supportive of life; hence the season is described as arriving “with the Vasus.”

The line “सं॒​वँ॒थ्स॒रस्य॑ सवि॒तुः” is then revisited: Samvatsara is the year; Savitṛ/Savitā is the Sun. The year belongs to Savitṛ; the Sun is the abhimāni devatā of the annual cycle. Earlier recordings have already established this identification. Here it underlines that the cycle of seasons, and the special position of Vasant as the first season, are under the “orders” of the Sun.

“प्रै॒ष॒कृ-त्प्र॑थ॒म-स्स्मृ॑तः” is parsed to mean that Vasant ṛtu, fulfilling the order of Savitṛ (praishakṛt), is remembered or regarded (smṛtaḥ) as “prathama” – the first of the six seasons. The teacher also notes that “prathama” can be read as “best,” so Vasant is both first in sequence and foremost in quality among the ṛtus.

The closing fragment, “अ॒मूना॒दय॑-तेत्य॒न्यान्,” is treated cautiously. “Amūn” is glossed as “these people,” “ādayata” as “take care of them,” and “anyān” as “others” or “sevakas” (helpers). The speaker explicitly says that the full sense of this line becomes clear only when it is read alongside the tenth pañcati, so he defers final interpretation. Provisionally, he takes it to be an instruction directed at sevakas to take care of certain people during Vasant ṛtu.

After unpacking the words, he steps back and summarizes what the ninth pañcati as a whole has covered. First, it completes the earlier warning that those who doubt śāstra cannot find the path to puṇya‑lokas. Second, it describes the structure of time in terms of ṛtus: each ṛtu has 60 days and 60 nights, each day having 30 muhūrtas and each night 30 muhūrtas, organized into Śukla and Kṛṣṇa pakṣas spread over two months. Numerically, this can be summarized as:

1 ṛtu = 60 days + 60 nights
1 full day (day + night) = 60 muhūrtas
Total muhūrtas in one ṛtu (full days) = 60 × 60 = 3600 muhūrtas

Third, the pañcati reinforces that ṛtus follow one another without gaps, each with its own rūpa (nature) and nāda (characteristic sound). Finally, Part 2 highlights Vasant ṛtu as the first and best season, arriving by the command of Savitṛ, clothed in red, bringing purified waters and accompanied by the Vasus, and it prepares the listener for the tenth pañcati, where the “अ॒मूना॒दय॑-तेत्य॒न्यान् ” line will be tied concretely to which people are to be cared for and how.
