सा॒रा॒ग॒व॒स्त्रै-र्ज॒रद॑क्षः । व॒स॒न्तो वसु॑भि-स्स॒ह । 
सं॒​वँ॒थ्स॒रस्य॑ सवि॒तुः । प्रै॒ष॒कृ-त्प्र॑थ॒म-स्स्मृ॑तः । अ॒मूना॒दय॑-तेत्य॒न्यान् ॥ 9 ॥
​
“सा॒रा॒ग॒व॒स्त्रै-र्ज॒रद॑क्षः, सा॒रा॒ग॒व॒स्त्रै:.” Literally rāga means coloured, the hue of a colour, colour. Vastraiḥ means garments, clothes. But here what it means is saragavastraiḥ. So the Ritu is compared or said as if it is covered with the red‑coloured clothes – the Ritu. So which Ritu is this one? They are talking about the Vasanta Ritu. So it starts describing the Vasanta Ritu first. So Vasanta Ritu looks as if it is covered with red‑coloured clothes.
​
“र्ज॒रद॑क्षः” And also it has – the waters in the Vasanta Ritu are very pure, clear; so र्ज॒रद॑क्षः – as if this Ritu is purifies those waters. It looks as if the Ritu, the Vasanta Ritu, purifies the waters. So it says: the Vasanta Ritu starts as if covered with red‑coloured clothes and comes with clean water sources – that’s what this literally means.
​
“व॒स॒न्तो वसु॑भि-स्स॒ह” Vasantaḥ – the spring season, the Vasanta Ritu. Vasubhiḥ – Vasubhiḥ is the Vasus, with the Vasus, Vasubhiḥ. So we know Aṣṭa Vasus are there in our śāstra; so it’s a group of all those deities. Vasus are the group of deities, devatās, who are responsible for the sukham of the jīvas or prajās; in general they take care of the welfare of the prajās – that’s the Vasus, Vasu devatās, Vasubhiḥ. There are Aṣṭa Vasus in our śāstra. So it comes with them; Vasanta Ritu comes with the Vasus. Saha – with them, it comes: Vasantaḥ Vasubhiḥ saha, it comes with the Vasus.
​
“सं॒​वँ॒थ्स॒रस्य॑ सवि॒तुः” Samvatsarasya – Samvatsaram we already saw, the year; Samvatsarasya – “of the year”. Savituḥ – Savituḥ, Savitrī devatā, Savituḥ – who is here nothing but the Sun. So Sun is the Abhimāni devatā for the Samvatsaram – we already saw that. So basically it says here it’s by his orders – the Vasanta Ritu comes by his order, by his order; whose order? The Sun devatā, Sūrya devatā.
​
“ प्रै॒ष॒कृ-त्प्र॑थ॒म-स्स्मृ॑तः” Praishakrit – Praishakrit means orders, fulfilling the orders, the orders of the Savitrī devatā we saw in the previous mantra; Praishakrit – fulfilling the orders. Prathama – first; this is the first Ritu of the six Ritus. Vasanta Ritu is the beginning or the start of the first Ritu, or we can say it’s the best Ritu. Prathamaḥ also we can interpret as “best”. Smṛtaḥ – it is remembered or considered; Smṛtaḥ – it is considered as the first Ritu or the best Ritu of the six Ritus.
​
“अ॒मूना॒दय॑-तेत्य॒न्यान् ” Amun – these people; Amun means “these people”. Ādayata – Ādayata means “take care of them, take care of these people”, Ādayata, or “these people take care of them”, Ādayata. Iti – thus; anyan – anyan means “others, others” or “sevakas”.
​
This meaning, this line, should be interpreted with the next Panchadi, 10th Panchadi, to get a full understanding of it. So we will defer the meaning for this mantra until the next Panchadi. Literally it is telling the sevakas, asking the sevakas, to take care of the people in the Vasanta Ritu. What kind of people? We’ll see those details in the next mantra. We’ll connect the last mantra with the 10th Panchadi and we’ll see them together.
​
So what this Panchadi says is: it concludes about the people who doubt śāstras, continuing with the previous Panchadi. It says those who doubt śāstras will not find a way to reach the Puṇya lokas. And then it switches the topic and it started telling or describing the Ritus itself. The Ritus are described here to have 60 days and nights (ṣaṣṭikau) in each Ritu. Each day is said to have 30 Muhurtas, and so is also night – 30 Muhurtas in a night also. So Ritus have 60 such days and they are equally spread into bright and dark – Śukla and Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa, two Śukla Pakṣam, two Kṛṣṇa Pakṣam. And the Ritus come one after each without any gap, as if the previous Ritu pulls in the next Ritu. Also the Ritus are described to have their own sound in this Panchadi.

And Vasanta Ritu is then described as the first Ritu, and in which we get the clean water sources. We saw in the previous Panchadi that Sūrya devatā is the Abhimāni devatā of the Samvatsaram. So here it says by his orders, the order of the Sūrya devatā, the Vasanta Ritu starts as if covered with red‑coloured clothes. And so it says “red‑coloured clothes” because the previous Ritu is Śiśira Ritu; after the Śiśira Ritu, Vasanta Ritu starts. And in the Vasanta Ritu we see all the flowers start blossoming, right? Things spring, life springs into action again, so that is considered and compared to the bright red colour – so that’s why it says “as if covered with red colour”. The Vasanta is considered as if it starts with red‑coloured clothes covering it.
​
And it comes with the Vasus – we saw that, Aṣṭa Vasus. So the Aṣṭa Vasus are Dhara – earth, Āpa – water, Anala – fire, Anila – wind, Pratyūṣa – dawn, Prabhāsa – light. So these are the Aṣṭa Vasus – earth, water, fire, wind, dawn and light, Prabhāsa. I’m missing two – Aṣṭa Vasus are Dhara, water, Anila, Āpa, Pratyūṣa, Prabhāsa; I’ll find the other two, I am forgetting that (Dhruva & Soma). So these Aṣṭa Vasus, they represent the natural elements. And after the Śiśira Ritu, the previous Ritu, in this Vasanta Ritu they are considered as more balanced in the Vasanta Ritu, and so it is said that the Vasanta Ritu comes with these devatās; it comes with these devatās.
​
So that’s what this ninth Panchadi is about. And the last mantra we will start the meaning connecting with the 10th Panchadi in the next recording.
