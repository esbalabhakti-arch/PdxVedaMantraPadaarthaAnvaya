गुरुर्ब्रह्मा गुरुर्विष्णुः
गुरुर्देवो महेश्वरः ।
गुरुः साक्षात्परब्रह्म
तस्मै श्रीगुरवे नमः ॥
गुरवे सर्वलोकानां भिषजे भवरोगिणाम् ।
निधये सर्वविद्यानां दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः ॥
अस्मिन् गुरु चरणारविन्दाभ्याम् नमः।​

Now let us chant the 10th Pañcati:
अ॒मूग्​श्च॑ परि॒रक्ष॑तः । ए॒ता वा॒चः प्र॑युज्य॒न्ते । 
यत्रै त॑दुप॒दृश्य॑ते ॥ ए॒तदे॒व वि॑जानी॒यात् । 
प्र॒माण॑-ङ्काल॒पर्य॑ये । वि॒शे॒ष॒ण-न्तु॑ वक्ष्या॒मः । 
ऋ॒तूना᳚-न्तन्नि॒बोध॑त ॥ शुक्लवासा॑ रुद्र॒गणः ।
 ग्री॒ष्मेणा॑वर्त॒ते स॑ह । नि॒जह॑-न्पृथि॑वीग्ं स॒र्वाम् ॥ 10 ॥

Now let us look at the line by line meaning.
“अ॒मूग्​श्च॑ परि॒रक्ष॑तः” – amūgh means “those”; here it is referring to the people who have come from far places. “Amūgh” – those who have come from far places. “Cha” – and. “Parirakṣata” – parirakṣ, protect them well, protect them well. So: protect those who have come from far places. Protect who and why, we will see in the next two mantras and we will connect them all together. 
We have to also take the very last line that we had in the previous pañcati: “अ॒मूना॒दय॑-तेत्य॒न्यान्” – we saw that in the last pañcati: “अ॒मूना॒दय॑-तेत्य॒न्यान्”. So we have to connect all these things together to understand the meaning of what it is trying to say.
So let us look at the second mantra in this 10th pañcati.
“ए॒ता वा॒चः प्र॑युज्य॒न्ते” – etā – these. Vācaḥ – words, words that are spoken, words or statements made. Prayujyate – are used, are used, prayujyate. So what it says is: words like these – “like these” meaning the one that we saw above, “protect well,” “take care of them,” “serve them,” that was in the previous pañcati last line – words like those are heard, are applied, are used, are heard. That is what this line means.
​
“यत्रै त॑दुप॒दृश्य॑ते” – yatra etat upadṛśyate. Yatra – where. Etat – this. Upadṛśyate – seen, upadṛśya – perceived or heard, is seen. So: when these words like the one that we saw are heard, know that this is Vasant ṛtu. When these kind of words are spoken or heard, know that it is Vasant ṛtu.
​
So connecting all these mantras including the last line from the previous pañcati – “अ॒मूना॒दय॑-तेत्य॒न्यान्, अ॒मूग्​श्च॑ परि॒रक्ष॑तः, ए॒ता वा॒चः प्र॑युज्य॒न्ते, यत्रै त॑दुप॒दृश्य॑ते ” – looking at these four mantras/lines, this is what they mean.

So mostly in the Vasant ṛtu, as spring blossoms, more activities happen – like marriages, upanayanam, etc. More activities, more festivals happen; they start happening in Vasant ṛtu. And this mantra says: the yajamānas who are doing those functions, the yajamānas of those functions, instruct their sevakas, their servants, or you can say instead of “instruct” – we can say they are telling their relatives or others – that all the relatives who come to attend the functions should be fed well; they all should be fed well. And those who have come from far places should be taken care of, should be well protected, should be taken care of properly. That is what it says.
​
And when these kinds of activities and words you hear – and you will be hearing them more and more, it says – you will be hearing more and more these kinds of words: “etā vācaḥ prayujyate” – these kinds of words will be used more and more in the Vasant ṛtu, that is what it says. And when such activities or such words are heard, understand that this is the Vasant ṛtu or the Vasant ṛtu has arrived. When you hear more of these words – “yatra etat upadṛśyate” – when you see such activities or when you hear such words, then you understand that the Vasant ṛtu has arrived. That is what this means.
​
Let us look at further what it says.
​
“ए॒तदे॒व वि॑जानी॒यात्” – etat eva vijānīyāt. Etat – this. Eva – indeed, alone. Vijānīyāt – one should know, one should understand; understand this. That is what this means: understand this alone, that is what literally this line means. But we will put them all together; we have to connect these three or four mantras together to get a real sense of what it is trying to say to us.
​
“प्र॒माण॑-ङ्काल॒पर्य॑ये ” – pramāṇam kāla‑paryaye. Pramāṇam means proof, authority, right, pramāṇam. Kāla‑paryaye – the lapse of time, kāla‑paryaye – the time that is gone, the lapse of time due to the ṛtus. We know every ṛtu has two months’ period, so the time lapse is what it is calling here.
​
“वि॒शे॒ष॒ण-न्तु॑ वक्ष्या॒मः” – viśeṣaṇam tu vakṣyāmaḥ. Viśeṣaṇam – specification, distinction; viśeṣaṇam means the distinction. Tu – indeed, indeed. Vakṣyāmaḥ – we shall explain, we will explain indeed the differences. Differences between what? Between the ṛtus. Kāla‑paryaye – when the time has lapsed, the next ṛtu comes; so we will explain the differences, viśeṣaṇam – what is the viśeṣaṇam of that particular ṛtu, we will explain (vakṣyāmaḥ) in the coming verses. We are going to explain those things.
​
“ऋ॒तूना᳚-न्तन्नि॒बोध॑त” – ṛtūnām tan nibodhata. Ṛtūnām – of the seasons. Viśeṣaṇam – we saw, “viśeṣaṇam” of the season; we have to connect here: “ṛtūnām viśeṣaṇam” – the viśeṣaṇam of the ṛtus, of the seasons. Tat nibodhata – tat nibodhata becomes “tannibodhata”. Tat – that. Nibodhata – understand. Nibodhata means understand, learn, understand.
​
So if we connect these four/five/six/seven – “ए॒तदे॒व वि॑जानी॒यात् । प्र॒माण॑-ङ्काल॒पर्य॑ये । वि॒शे॒ष॒ण-न्तु॑ वक्ष्या॒मः । ऋ॒तूना᳚-न्तन्नि॒बोध॑त ” – it says: all these four mantras kind of generalize the nature or guṇas of the ṛtu. It says: the Vasant ṛtu has thus been described; we have described Vasant ṛtu in the previous few mantras – “etat eva vijānīyāt” – we have described it, or you have to understand the way it has been described; Vasant ṛtu has been described, understand that. And it says: know that there are properties or guṇas that are distinct from each ṛtu; they are not the same. “Viśeṣaṇam tu vakṣyāmaḥ” – we are going to explain those distinctions between the ṛtus, which we are going to explain in the coming verse, and understand that there are differences between ṛtus which we are going to explain – “viśeṣaṇam tu vakṣyāmaḥ ṛtūnām tat nibodhata”. So we are going to explain those differences of the ṛtus in the following verses.
​
“शुक्लवासा॑ रुद्र॒गणः,ग्री॒ष्मेणा॑वर्त॒ते स॑ह” – śukla‑vāsā rudra‑gaṇaḥ gṛīṣmeṇa āvartate saha. Now it is going to explain the next ṛtu, next ṛtu which is Grīṣma ṛtu. “Śukla‑vāsā” – wearing white garments; śukla‑vāsā means something that is wearing white garments, wearing white garments, white clothes. “Rudra‑gaṇaḥ” – who, who is wearing those things? Rudra‑gaṇaḥ. So we know there are Rudras, a host or group of Rudras; there are 11 Rudras we know, right? So Rudra‑gaṇas with white garments. “Gṛīṣmeṇa āvartate saha” – gṛīṣmeṇa āvartate saha; gṛīṣmeṇa – with the summer or the summer season, Grīṣma ṛtu. “Āvartate” – proceeds, comes, turns. “Saha” – together with. So the Grīṣma ṛtu comes with the 11 Rudras who are wearing white garments – that is what these two mantras mean: “śukla‑vāsā rudra‑gaṇaḥ gṛīṣmeṇa āvartate saha”.
​
So after the Vasant ṛtu, Grīṣma ṛtu proceeds, āvartate, comes; comes with Rudra‑gaṇas wearing white garments.

“नि॒जह॑-न्पृथि॑वीग्ं स॒र्वाम्” – nijahan pṛthvī sarvām. Nijahan – nijahan means burning, burning; due to the heat, because Grīṣma ṛtu represents heat, the sun‑rays are very hot, so the burning – nijahan. Pṛthvī sarvā – the entire earth, the whole place, everything, all the place; the earth, pṛthvī sarvam, the entire pṛthvī, all the earth is burning hot, means very hot – nijahan – because of the start of the Grīṣma ṛtu.
​
So putting together 8, 9, 10 (i.e., these lines), it describes the Grīṣma ṛtu – that it comes covered with white‑coloured clothes and with 11 Rudra‑gaṇas, and as it comes, it is burning heat, burning heat, and it burns the entire earth with its radiation.
​
So this is a very small pañcati; in this pañcati it summarizes the Vasant ṛtu first, where we have a lot of activities where people take care of each other, they feed others, and then they protect those who have come from far. Protecting means upacāra – just taking care of them, right? And when such activities are heard more, you understand that it is Vasant ṛtu. And it also says each ṛtu is distinct from the other ṛtus, and we are going to describe them in the following verses.

And it starts describing the next ṛtu, which is the Grīṣma ṛtu, which comes with 11 Rudra‑gaṇas who are covered in white‑coloured garments or clothes, and its guṇa is burning heat – that is what this pañcati explains.

Now, the Rudra‑gaṇas – 11 forms of Rudra‑gaṇas – are pretty different depending on where you pick the names from. For example, from Viṣṇu Purāṇa, if you take it, the 11 Rudras are defined as: Kapālī, Piṅgala, Bhīma, Virūpākṣa, Vilohita, Ajeśa, Ahirbudhnya, Śambhu, Caṇḍa, Bhava, Rudra – these are the 11 forms; they are given in Viṣṇu Purāṇa. But if same thing, Mahābhārata also describes 11 forms of Rudra, where Bhīma worshipped – not Bhīṣma, Bhīma worshipped. So it says: Hara, Bahurūpa, Trayambaka, Aparājita, Vṛṣakapi, Śambhu, Kapardī, Raivata, Mṛgavyādha, Sarva, Kapālī – few common names between those two lists, but these are the 11 forms of Rudras from the Purāṇas.

Now, putting together these two ṛtus, what we have seen so far: Vasant ṛtu – see, if we remember, it comes with red‑coloured clothes, with Aṣṭa‑Vasus (eight Vasus), and it provides us clean waters. And the Grīṣma ṛtu comes with 11 Rudras, covered with white‑coloured clothes, but its guṇa is burning heat.

Now we will see the other ṛtus, how they are described, in the next pañcati onwards.

पूर्णस्य पूर्णमादाय पूर्णमेवावशिष्यते ॥
ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः ॥
