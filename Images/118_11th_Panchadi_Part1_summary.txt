The 11th pañcati begins by concluding the description of Grīṣma ṛtu (summer) and then transitions into a detailed and layered explanation of Varṣā ṛtu (rainy season). The opening line, “jyotiṣā apratikṣyeṇa saḥ,” emphasizes the intense and irresistible radiance (tejas) of the Rudra-gaṇas, which was introduced in the previous pañcati. This serves as a final reinforcement that the scorching heat of summer is not merely physical but is symbolically attributed to the powerful, unobstructed energy of the eleven Rudras.

With this closure, the text shifts focus to Varṣā ṛtu. The next lines introduce the Dvādaśa Ādityas (twelve solar deities) as the governing forces behind this season. The phrase “viśvarūpāṇi vāsāṃsi” portrays them as wearing garments of many forms and colors, symbolizing the diversity and richness of natural manifestations during the rainy season. These “garments” can be interpreted as the various visual expressions of nature—clouds, rainbows, lush greenery, and changing skies—reflecting the multiplicity of forms through which the Ādityas operate.

A central teaching emerges in the line “saṃvatsareṇam karma-phalam varṣābhiḥ dadhat.” This establishes that the rains of Varṣā ṛtu are directly responsible for sustaining the fruits of actions throughout the entire year. In simple terms, whatever effort is invested during this season—especially agricultural activity—becomes the foundation for year-long sustenance. Thus, Varṣā ṛtu is not just another season but a critical phase that determines the success or failure of the entire yearly cycle.

The next set of verses provides a nuanced experiential description of the rainy season. It is described as “adukkha” (not painful), emphasizing the relief it brings after the harsh summer. The cool winds and rains create a soothing environment that removes the discomfort caused by heat. However, this pleasantness is not presented as absolute. The text introduces a subtle duality: the season appears “duḥkha-cakṣur iva” (as if having sorrowful eyes) and “pīta iva dṛśyate” (as if tinged with yellow). These descriptions are interpreted as references to common seasonal ailments such as eye infections or fevers, which give the outward impression of suffering despite the overall beneficial nature of the season.

At the same time, Varṣā ṛtu is depicted as highly nourishing, especially for animals. The phrase “rurudakṣa iva dṛśyate” is understood as the season appearing like a nourisher of creatures, particularly forest animals like deer. With abundant rainfall, vegetation flourishes, ensuring that animals have sufficient food and thrive during this period.

Thus, the portrayal of Varṣā ṛtu is deliberately balanced. It is a season of relief, nourishment, and productivity, yet it also carries minor discomforts and health challenges. This dual nature reinforces a deeper insight: natural cycles are rarely one-dimensional; they contain both benefit and limitation.

Overall, this first part of the 11th pañcati establishes Varṣā ṛtu as a life-sustaining and action-dependent season, governed by divine forces (Ādityas), characterized by abundance and diversity, and essential for ensuring the continuity of life and the fruition of human effort across the year.
