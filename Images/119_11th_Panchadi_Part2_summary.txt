The second part of the 11th pañcati deepens the understanding of Varṣā ṛtu by shifting from description to principle and consequence, highlighting how human action during this season determines long-term outcomes.

The opening phrase, “hlādayate jvalataś caiva,” conveys that the rainy season brings both happiness and radiance. The term hlādayate indicates that it gives joy and comfort to all beings, while jvalataḥ refers to a form of brilliance or vitality (tejas). This is not the harsh heat of summer but a life-giving radiance that enables growth and renewal. The next line, “śāmyataś cāsya cakṣuṣī,” introduces a powerful metaphor: Varṣā ṛtu is visualized as a puruṣa (cosmic person) whose “eyes” bring calmness and remove discomfort. These “eyes” symbolize the rains and cooling effects that soothe the earth after the intensity of summer.

The teaching then becomes more direct and practical. The lines “yāvai prajā bhramśyante saṃvatsarāt tā bhramśyante” state that those who fail to act during this season are considered as having “fallen away” not just from the season, but from the entire year. This is a profound statement. It emphasizes that neglecting timely action—such as a farmer failing to sow seeds—has consequences that extend far beyond the immediate moment. The loss is not temporary; it affects the entire annual cycle.

In contrast, the next lines present the complementary principle: “yāḥ pratitiṣṭhanti saṃvatsare tāḥ pratitiṣṭhanti.” Those who act appropriately during Varṣā ṛtu are considered “established” throughout the year. Their efforts during this critical window ensure stability, productivity, and sustained benefit. The concluding phrase “varṣābhya ity arthaḥ” clarifies that this entire outcome—both success and failure—arises because of the rains.

This section therefore transforms the description of Varṣā ṛtu into a law of timing and action. The season is not merely beneficial by itself; it becomes beneficial only when aligned with proper human effort. Nature provides the opportunity, but it is human responsiveness that determines the result.

The summary portion at the end of the text consolidates the teachings of the entire 11th pañcati. It recalls the earlier completion of Grīṣma ṛtu, where the intense heat was attributed to the Rudra-gaṇas, and then highlights the key features of Varṣā ṛtu: the role of the twelve Ādityas, the abundance of rains, the nourishment of vegetation and animals, and the mixed experiential qualities (comfort alongside minor ailments).

Most importantly, the pañcati emphasizes a central philosophical insight: right action at the right time sustains the entire cycle of life. Varṣā ṛtu becomes the decisive phase where effort translates into long-term benefit. The imagery of the season as a puruṣa further reinforces its living, dynamic nature—it sees, nourishes, and responds.

Thus, this second part elevates the discussion from observation to instruction, teaching that alignment with natural cycles—especially timely action during crucial periods—is essential for prosperity, stability, and continuity throughout the year.
