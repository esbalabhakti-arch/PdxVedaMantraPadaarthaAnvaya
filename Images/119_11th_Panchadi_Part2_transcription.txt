ह्लादयते᳚ ज्वल॑तश्चै॒व । शा॒म्यत॑श्चास्य॒ चक्षु॑षी । 
यावै प्रजा भ्र॑ग्ग्​श्य॒न्ते । सं​वँथ्सरात्ता भ्र॑ग्ग्​श्य॒न्ते ॥ 
याः॒ प्रति॑तिष्ठ॒न्ति । सं​वँथ्सरे ताः प्रति॑तिष्ठ॒न्ति । 
व॒र्॒षाभ्य॑ इत्य॒र्थः 

“ह्लादयते᳚ ज्वल॑तश्चै॒व” Hlādayate means “giving pleasure,” pleasure‑giving; Hlādayate – that gives pleasure. Jvalataḥ means burning, or kānti, tejas; “ca eva” – indeed, also, indeed. So: “hlādayate jvalataś ca eva”.
​
“शा॒म्यत॑श्चास्य॒ चक्षु॑षी” Samyataḥ – calm, calmness, removing any uncomfortableness. “Cha” – and; “asya cakṣuṣī” – his eyes. Whose eyes here? Now here, the mantra describes Varṣā ṛtu as a puruṣa, as a person, and then addresses his eyes – asya cakṣuṣī: through his eyes he is bringing the comfort, samyataḥ.
​
“यावै प्रजा भ्र॑ग्ग्​श्य॒न्ते” Ye – those; vai – indeed; prajāḥ – the beings; bhramśyante – perish, fall away, fall away, perish or fall away.
​
“सं​वँथ्सरात्ता भ्र॑ग्ग्​श्य॒न्ते” Saṃvatsarāt – from the year, from the saṃvatsara; te – they; bhramśyante – perish or fall away.
​
So what is conveyed in these four mantras – “ह्लादयते᳚ ज्वल॑तश्चै॒व । शा॒म्यत॑श्चास्य॒ चक्षु॑षी । यावै प्रजा भ्र॑ग्ग्​श्य॒न्ते । सं​वँथ्सरात्ता भ्र॑ग्ग्​श्य॒न्ते ॥ ”?
​
In the Varṣā ṛtu, we know that it gives happiness to all – Hlādayate, it gives happiness to all. But those people who do not act in this ṛtu – like a farmer forgetting to sow the seeds in this ṛtu – “यावै प्रजा भ्र॑ग्ग्​श्य॒न्ते,” those who forget to act will surely be considered as “fallen from this year”: that means they will suffer through the year, they will not reap the benefit if they do not act in this ṛtu. And “सं​वँथ्सरात्ता भ्र॑ग्ग्​श्य॒न्ते” – not just in this ṛtu, but they are considered as fallen from the entire year, saṃvatsarāt. Because they did not act, they will not reap the benefit for the entire year.
​
Why is that? Because it says “samyatas chāsya cakṣuṣī”: the Varṣā ṛtu, through his eyes – when compared as a puruṣa – through his eyes he is bringing, he removes the uncomfortableness. And because of his tejas, kānti, all the trees, everything that flourishes on this earth – plants, herbs, trees – they all shine forth because of his kānti, tejas: “jvalataś caiva,” we said “ह्लादयते᳚ ज्वल॑तश्चै॒व” He brings this happiness through his kānti, and the kānti is expressed through his eyes – asya cakṣuṣī. That is what these four lines mean.
​
Let’s look at the other three lines.

“याः॒ प्रति॑तिष्ठ॒न्ति” Yaḥ – those; pratitishthanti – remain established.
​
“सं​वँथ्सरे ताः प्रति॑तिष्ठ॒न्ति” Saṃvatsare – in the year; tāḥ – they; pratitishthanti – remain established.
​
“व॒र्॒षाभ्य॑ इत्य॒र्थः” Varṣābhyaḥ – from the rains; “iti” – thus; arthaḥ – meaning.
​
So putting together these last three lines – “याः॒ प्रति॑तिष्ठ॒न्ति । सं​वँथ्सरे ताः प्रति॑तिष्ठ॒न्ति । व॒र्॒षाभ्य॑ इत्य॒र्थः” – it gives the complementary meaning to the previous four lines where we saw those who do not act are considered fallen away. Here it says: those who indeed act, those indeed who act – like a farmer who sows his seed at the right time in this ṛtu – they will benefit; they are considered as established, pratitishthanti. They are considered established; they will reap the benefit not just for the ṛtu but “saṃvatsare tāḥ pratitishthanti” – throughout the year they will reap the benefit of it. “Varṣābhya ity arthaḥ” – thus, because of (or “from”) the rains.
​
So: what is that? This Varṣā ṛtu brings the rains; because of the rains in the Varṣā ṛtu, when they act at the right time and in the right season, they benefit because of this ṛtu – the entire year they benefit. That is what these lines mean.

So with this, the third anuvāka is over. So with the 11th pañcati, the third anuvāka is over.

Now let us see, together, all the mantras put together – what does this 11th pañcati tell us? First it finished up the Grīṣma ṛtu definition; it says that the heat or burning hotness in Grīṣma ṛtu is due to the tejas of the Rudra‑gaṇas – that is what it said. Then it finished that definition of Grīṣma ṛtu.

Then it starts to describe the Varṣā ṛtu: the Varṣā ṛtu comes with twelve Ādityas who are covered in different clothings; plenty of rain that provides benefits for the entire year. Also, at the beginning, it appears as if the ṛtu brings forth diseases like red eyes, yellow fever, etc., even though this ṛtu is very blessed and very comfortable unlike Grīṣma ṛtu. Also, the cold wind that blows in Varṣā ṛtu is not that unpleasant compared to the winter season, for example.

And the rains in this season provide enough nourishment to animals like deer, etc.; it brings forth happiness to all. And those who work hard – like sowing the seeds at the right time – will reap the benefits for the entire year, and those who do not will suffer for the entire year. So Varṣā ṛtu is compared to a puruṣa, and these mantras explain that through his eyes he makes the vegetation grow and removes the discomfort we had in Grīṣma ṛtu, like relieving us from the heat, etc. That is the essence of this 11th pañcati.
