The 14th Panchādi of the Āruṇa Praśnam reveals Śiśira Ṛtu — the coldest, driest phase of winter — as a season of profound scarcity. The skies (देवलोकेषु), once heavy with monsoon clouds, are now empty and barren, offering no water. Among humans (मनूनाम्), water is found only inside homes — rivers and open sources have dried up, forcing people to seek water door to door. Those who wander in search of it move like flashes of lightning (वैद्युतः), restless and urgent, speaking words of longing and lack. Witnessing this suffering, Agni Devatā observes that जीविका — the very sustenance of life — is absent from the earth. Moved by deep compassion, he turns to the Marut Devatās — the same wind gods who brought the biting cold of Hemanta — and offers a heartfelt prayer. He addresses them as सूर्यत्वचः, those whose nature shines with the brilliance of the Sun, and implores them to send शर्म सप्रथा — abundant, widespread protection in the form of rain — everywhere across the parched earth. Thus Śiśira, though marked by hardship, is ultimately a season of divine intercession, where suffering becomes the seed of renewal.
