The 18th panchadi serves as the conclusion to the discussion on *Shishira ritu* (the winter season) while simultaneously synthesizing the concepts of all previous *ritus* (seasons) to describe the Sun (*Surya*) as *Kalasvaroopi*, the embodiment of time. The text opens by addressing those who may not know how to conduct themselves or survive during the harsh cold of the winter season. It emphasizes physical behavioral adaptations required for the season through the phrase निजानुकामे᳚ न्यञ्ज॒लिका. This refers to kneeling with one's knees touching the ground and folding the hands tightly together in an *Anjali mudra*. This specific posture is explained as a practical method to preserve body heat and keep oneself warm during the freezing weather. Through the line अमी वाच-मुपास॑तामि॒ति, the text stresses that people must listen to, respect, and strictly follow these spoken words and behavioral instructions regarding the winter season.

Moving forward, the text shifts from seasonal survival to the spiritual and cosmic rewards of understanding the natural order. It states that for an individual who truly understands the boundaries, limits, and specific characteristics of all the seasons—referred to as *Maryadha Karatvaat*—the seasons themselves will yield. The phrase तस्मै सर्व ऋतवो॑ नम॒न्ते signifies that all the seasons will bow down to and worship such a person. Furthermore, anyone who holds this profound cosmic knowledge (य ए॑वं-वेँ॒द) will attain *Brahmanatvam*, the state of supreme realization, achieving a preeminent and foremost position (मर्यादा करत्वा-त्प्र॑पुरो॒धाम् । ब्राह्मण॑ आप्नो॒ति). The text explicitly clarifies that the term *Brahmana* in this context does not denote a specific social sect or caste; rather, it refers strictly to any enlightened individual who understands that all these seasonal characteristics emanate directly from the Sun as the ultimate controller of time.

The final portion of the panchadi explains the relationship between the Sun God, the cosmic deities, and the enlightened individual. The line स खलु सं​वँथ्सर एतै-स्सेनानी॑भि-स्स॒ह refers to the Sun God acting as the ultimate *Kalasvaroopi* (*Samvatsara*). The Sun operates in unison with various cosmic leaders and companion deities (*Senanibhi*) that were detailed in preceding sections, including the *Ashtavasus*, the twelve *Adityas*, and the eleven *Rudras*. Together with these cosmic forces, the Sun brings about the fulfillment of all desires. In the culminating phrase इन्द्राय सर्वान्-कामान॑भिव॒हति, the word *Indraya* does not refer to the king of gods, but metaphorically represents the realized seeker or *Brahmana* who has mastered and brought all of their physical senses under strict control. For such a self-controlled individual, the Sun God ensures that all desires are entirely brought to them, leading to a state of complete spiritual satisfaction and fulfillment.
