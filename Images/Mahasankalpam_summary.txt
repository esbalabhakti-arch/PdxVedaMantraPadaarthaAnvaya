The primary distinction between the Laghu Sankalpam and the Maha Sankalpam is the scope of environmental description. The Laghu version focuses almost exclusively on temporal specifications, defining the moment through cosmic cycles such as the Kalpa, Manvantara, and Yuga, down to the specific year, season, and lunar day (Tithi). It identifies the practitioner’s position primarily in relation to time.

The Maha Sankalpam significantly expands this by including detailed geographic and cosmological locations. It describes the universe as a series of layers, citing seven worlds below the earth (Atala, Vitala, etc.) and six worlds above (Bhuvarloka, Swarloka, etc.). It situates the practitioner within a specific Dwipa (continent) and Varsha (sub-continent), noting that while India is in Jambudwipa/Bharata Varsha, the speaker identifies the Americas as Kraunchadwipa/Ramanaka Varsha.

Furthermore, the Maha Sankalpam catalogs the physical features of the earth to pinpoint the ritual site. This includes the Sapta Sagaras (seven types of waters/oceans), major mountain ranges like the Himalayas and Vindhyas, and sacred forests such as Dandakaranya. It also lists specific holy rivers, including the Ganga, Yamuna, and Kaveri, as well as ancient sacred cities (Punya Puris) and pilgrimage sites (Kshetrams).

Ultimately, while the Laghu Sankalpam establishes the "when," the Maha Sankalpam exhaustively establishes both the "when" and the "where." It creates a complete picture of the practitioner's location within the cosmic waters, the specific island-continent, and the local topography of mountains, rivers, and forests.
