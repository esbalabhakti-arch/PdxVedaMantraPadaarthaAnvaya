This text provides a specialized extension of the Maha Sankalpam, focusing on the sophisticated layers of temporal classification and the methodologies used to measure time within the Vedic system.

Hierarchical Eras: Kalpas and Manvantaras The Maha Sankalpam explicitly identifies seven Kalpas (aeons): Parthiva, Kurma, Pralayananta, Shweta Varaha, Brahma, and Savitri. The current period is identified as the Shweta Varaha Kalpa. Additionally, the text lists seven Manvantaras (ages of Manu) and their corresponding rulers, including Swayambhuva, Swarochisha, Uttama, Tamasa, Raivata, Chakshusha, and the current Vaivasvata.

Systems of Time Measurement (Navamanas) A significant feature of the Sankalpam is the acknowledgment of nine distinct methods of time measurement (Navamanas), ranging from cosmic to terrestrial scales. These include:

Brahma: Based on cosmic cycles of the creator.

Daiva & Pitrya: Pertaining to the gods and ancestors.

Barhaspatya: Measured via the orbital revolution of Jupiter (Brihaspati) around the earth.

Saura & Chandra: Solar and lunar-based measurements.

Other systems: Prajapatya, Savana, and Nakshatra.

Integrated Application in Ritual In practice, both the Laghu and Maha Sankalpams utilize a dual system known as Saura Chandramana Dwayena. This integrates solar and lunar calendars to specify the current date. Under this framework, the Masam (month) is determined by solar or lunar standards, Paksham (fortnight) and Nakshatram (star) are derived from lunar calculations, and Vasaram (day of the week) is derived from solar calculations.

Cyclical Year Designation Finally, the text highlights the use of a 60-year cycle starting with the year Prabhava. The Sankalpam specifies the practitioner's current position within this cycle; the example provided identifies the current year as Vishwavasu Samvatsara.
