Hierarchical Structure of Cosmic Time
The Saṅkalpam begins at the macro level, establishing the practitioner's place within the vast timeline of Hindu cosmology.


The Age of Brahma: The declaration begins with Adya brahmaṇaḥ, referring to the current creator deity. It specifies that we are in the dvitīya parārdhe, or the second half of Brahma's life, meaning his first 50 years have concluded and he is currently in his 51st year.

The Kalpam and Manvantara: The timeline narrows to the Śvetavarāha Kalpa, a cosmic day equivalent to 1,000 catur-yugams. Within this, we reside in the Vaivasvata Manvantara.


The Yuga Cycle: The practitioner identifies the current era as the 28th catur-yugam. Having passed through the first three yugas, we are now in the prathame pāde (first quarter) of the Kaliyuga.

Spatial and Geographical Localization
Following the cosmic timeline, the Saṅkalpam transitions to a geographical location on Earth, moving from the continent to the specific landmass.


Continental Divisions: Traditionally, rituals performed in India utilize the term Jambūdvīpe. However, for practitioners in America, the terminology shifts to Krauñcadvīpe.


Topographic Orientation: The location is further refined relative to the sacred Mount Meru, specifically Meroḥ dakṣiṇe pārśve (to the south of Meru) for the Indian landmass.

Transactional and Calendaric Details
The final portion of the Saṅkalpam is described as vyavahārike, shifting from the cosmic to the "transactional" or human level of time-keeping.


The 60-Year Cycle: The calendar operates on a repeating 60-year cycle (Prabhavādi), with each year possessing a unique name, such as Viśvavasu.


Solar and Lunar Integration: Time is measured through a combination of Sauramānam (solar) and Chandramānam (lunar) systems. This includes:


Ayanams: The half-yearly movement of the sun (Uttarāyaṇam or Dakṣiṇāyaṇam).


Seasons and Months: The identification of the current Ṛtu (season) and Māsa (month), which may vary between solar and lunar basis.


Lunar Cycles: Precise details include the Pakṣam (fortnight), Tithi (lunar day), and Nakṣatram (star).

Ritual Context and Variation
The language used in the Saṅkalpam is adaptive to the nature of the ritual being performed.


Auspicious vs. Funerary: Standard auspicious rites begin with Śubhe śobhane muhūrte. In contrast, ancestral rites (pitṛ-kāryaṅgal) omit this phrase, often beginning with purificatory verses like Apavitraḥ pavitro vā.

Specific Alignments: Certain rituals require precise celestial alignments. For example, Yamatarpaṇam must coincide with a Tuesday (Bhaumavāsara), the Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa, and the 14th lunar day (Caturdaśī).
